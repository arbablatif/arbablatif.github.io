RAG Evaluation: Chunks, Embeddings and Top-k

A controlled experiment comparing retrieval-augmented generation design choices on one document set and one question set: 3 chunk sizes, 2 embedding models and 2 top-k values, giving 12 configurations.

Problem

RAG systems are usually built on defaults: a chunk size from a tutorial, a popular embedding model and a top-k of 5. Those choices decide what the model sees, but teams rarely test them. This lab replaces guesswork with a repeatable comparison that reports its own uncertainty.

Approach

  • Five open-access arXiv papers and 20 hand-written questions, including 4 unanswerable ones and a near miss.
  • The prompt, overlap, search method and LLM stay fixed, so only the three factors change.
  • Hit rate is matched on verbatim evidence snippets, which is stricter than matching a page.
  • A blind LLM judge scores answers against a written rubric, and the judge itself was audited.
  • 95% bootstrap intervals and paired comparisons show which gaps can be trusted.

Results

  • Only the top-k effect on hit rate clears its interval. Other results point in a consistent direction but are evidence, not proof.
  • The system fails by refusing, not by inventing: all 48 unanswerable cases were correctly declined, and none of the 143 real answers was entirely wrong.
  • Retrieval decides the outcome. Answers were fully correct 93% of the time after a hit and 11% after a miss.
  • The judge agreed with deterministic fact matching on 94.4% of answers, and all 6 disagreements were read by hand.

Limitations

  • With 16 answerable questions, one question moves a score by about 6 points.
  • The corpus is 5 machine learning papers, so results may differ for contracts or manuals.
  • One LLM and one run; the judge is the same model as the generator.
  • Rerankers, hybrid search and semantic chunking were not tested.
